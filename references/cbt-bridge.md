# Stoicism and CBT

Modern cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) openly credits Stoic ideas. Aaron
Beck and Albert Ellis both cited Epictetus. Donald Robertson's work is the
best accessible bridge between the two. Use this file when the user is
stuck in worry, rumination, or harsh self-criticism.

This is a summary in our own words, not a quote from any modern author.
Stoa is not therapy. Use these as thinking tools, not treatment.

## The shared core
Both traditions hold that our emotional responses are shaped by how we
interpret events. Change the interpretation and the feeling shifts.
Epictetus: it is our judgments that disturb us. CBT: thoughts, feelings, and
behaviors influence each other.

## Mapping

| Stoic practice | CBT counterpart |
|---|---|
| Examine the impression | Noticing automatic thoughts |
| Sorting what is up to me | Separating controllable from uncontrollable; problem-solving vs acceptance |
| Premeditatio malorum | Imaginal exposure and coping rehearsal |
| Evening review | Thought records and daily reflection |
| View from above | Cognitive distancing and perspective-taking |
| Voluntary discomfort | Behavioral experiments and graded exposure |
| Contemplating the sage | Modeling and values-based action |

## Cognitive distortions the Stoics would recognize
- **Catastrophizing**: "This is unbearable." Ask: Is it terrible, or merely
  unwelcome? Have I borne worse?
- **Mind reading**: assuming what others think. Their opinions are not up to
  us and often are not what we imagine.
- **All-or-nothing thinking**: one lapse as total failure. The Stoic is a
  "prokopton" (one making progress), not a finished sage.
- **Should statements**: rigid demands on self, others, or the world. Compare
  with wishing events to happen as they do.
- **Personalization**: taking blame for what was outside our control.

## Socratic questions to borrow
- What is the evidence for this thought, and against it?
- Is this in my control? What part is?
- What would I say to a friend who thought this?
- What is a more accurate and more useful way to see it?
- What is one action in line with my values I can take today?

## Where to be careful
Persistent low mood, panic, trauma reactions, or thoughts of self-harm call
for a professional. Say so kindly. Do not use Stoic framing to minimize what
the user is going through.

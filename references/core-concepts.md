# Core Stoic concepts (in plain words)

A working glossary for Stoa. These are summaries in our own words. For
anything the user will see in quotation marks, use the lookup script on the
bundled texts.

## The dichotomy of control
Some things are up to us: our judgments, intentions, choices, and effort.
Others are not: other people's actions, outcomes, our health, reputation,
the past. Peace and good character come from putting our care where we have
power. Epictetus opens the Enchiridion with this idea.

## Judgments, not events
We are disturbed less by events than by the stories and value-judgments we
attach to them. The practice is to pause and examine the impression: "You
are an impression, and not at all the thing you appear to be."

## Impressions, assent, and first movements
A flash of fear or anger arrives before we choose anything. That is not a
failure. What matters is whether we assent to the judgment that comes with
it ("this is terrible and I cannot bear it"). Seneca and Epictetus both
discuss this.

## The four virtues
Wisdom (seeing clearly), justice (fairness toward others), courage (doing
what is right despite fear), and temperance (self-command). For the Stoics
virtue is the only true good, and everything else is an "indifferent."

## Preferred indifferents
Health, wealth, reputation, and comfort are not bad to want. They are
"preferred." But they do not decide whether we live well, so we hold them
loosely and do not trade our character for them.

## Roles and duties
We live in relationships (parent, partner, colleague, citizen). Epictetus
and Hierocles stress acting well in each role. Stoicism is not withdrawal.

## Premeditatio malorum
Rehearse difficulties in advance so they do not blindside us and so we can
decide how we will act. Not gloom, preparation.

## Amor fati and the reserve clause
Welcome what happens as the material for practicing virtue. When pursuing a
goal, hold it "with reservation" ("I'll do this, if nothing prevents it"),
so that outcomes outside our control do not break us.

## The view from above
Zoom out to the scale of the city, the planet, and deep time to see
worries in proportion. Marcus uses this repeatedly.

## Memento mori
Remember death as a reason to live and act well now, not as morbidity.

## Prosoche
Attention: the continual watchfulness over our own thoughts and actions.
Pierre Hadot treats it as the foundation of Stoic practice.

## Common misreadings to gently correct
- "Stoic" does not mean unfeeling. The goal is clear judgment, not numbness.
- Accepting what we cannot control is not passivity about what we can.
- Stoicism does not excuse staying in harm.
